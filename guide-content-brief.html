Write a programme brief before producing the campaign

A good brief connects academic substance with a clear audience decision. It gives writers, designers and producers the same starting point without prescribing every creative detail.

A Merit Way editorial guide · 21 September 2026

In this guide
  1. Choose one audience and one action
  2. Separate the promise from the proof
  3. Give the production team useful constraints
  4. Agree how you will learn from the launch

Choose one audience and one action

Describe a real audience situation: a working professional comparing part-time study, for example, rather than “everyone interested in education”. Identify the decision they are ready to make now. A new audience may need an explanation or a guide before being ready to enquire. Set one primary action for each piece of content so the message does not compete with itself.

Separate the promise from the proof

Write the main benefit in plain language, then list the evidence that supports it: curriculum, teaching approach, assessment, project work or a verified instructor background. Check every factual claim with the programme owner. Avoid treating a recognised institutional name as evidence for an unrelated outcome. If the creative idea depends on a claim you cannot substantiate, revise the idea before production.

Give the production team useful constraints

Specify the channels, formats, languages, delivery dates and approval owner. Include the essential practical details learners will ask about. For video, identify available speakers, locations and the material needed to show the learning rather than only describe it. A concise interview outline with a clear editorial purpose often helps more than a script full of promotional adjectives.

Agree how you will learn from the launch

Choose a measure aligned with the intended action: useful enquiries, completed information requests or engagement with a detailed programme page. Review the questions people actually ask. Repeated confusion about eligibility or format is an editorial signal to improve the page. Keep a versioned brief so that later campaign pieces use the same facts and benefit from what the first release taught you.

Put it into practice: choose one idea from this guide and turn it into a short working note for your next decision.
