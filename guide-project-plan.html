PROJECT PRACTICE

A one-page project plan that people can actually use

A useful plan makes the next decision easier. For a small launch or media project, begin with a shared definition of the result before building a detailed schedule.

A Merit Way editorial guide · 21 September 2026

In this guide
  1. Define what done looks like
  2. Make scope and ownership visible
  3. Plan milestones around decisions
  4. Use the plan in a weekly conversation

Define what done looks like

Write the intended result in concrete terms: a bilingual programme page approved and published by a named date, for example. List the deliverables and identify who accepts them. Keep activities separate from outcomes; “hold three meetings” says little about whether the project has succeeded. Add two or three acceptance checks so the team can recognise completion without a new debate.

Make scope and ownership visible

Name the work included in this phase and the work deferred to later. Assign one accountable owner to each deliverable, even if several people contribute. Identify the reviewer and the information they need. If a team is waiting on an expert biography or a course outline, show that dependency explicitly; hidden dependencies turn a neat timeline into a misleading promise.

Plan milestones around decisions

Use a few milestones such as brief agreed, first draft reviewed, production approved and launch checked. Give each a date and a decision owner. Keep a small risk register with the risk, an early warning sign and the response. For a programme campaign, late changes to fees or start dates might require a final information check before publication.

Use the plan in a weekly conversation

Review three things: what changed, what is blocked and which decision is needed next. Update the plan while the team is present. At completion, compare the delivered result with the original checks and capture one improvement for next time. The value of the page comes from maintaining a shared understanding, not from making the document look elaborate.

Put it into practice: choose one idea from this guide and turn it into a short working note for your next decision.
